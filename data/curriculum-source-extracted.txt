7. Background Information
7.1 Aim of the Programme 
The Bachelor of Technology (BTech) in Computer Science aims at providing students with a
thorough grounding in both the theoretical and practical aspects of Computer Science, necessary
for successful career progression in the continually developing digital world.
7.2 Objectives of the Programme
The objectives of the programme are to produce graduates that will be to: 
1. critically  identify,  analyze  and  provide  solutions  to  real-world  problems  using
innovation and creativity through advanced techniques and tools in different areas of
computing. 
2. demonstrate proficiency in programming languages, operating systems and computer
architecture.
3. modify established models, techniques, algorithms, data structures, etc. to efficiently
solve new problems.

===== PAGE 3 =====
4. design, implement and evaluate computer-based systems and applications to meet the
desired needs using modern tools and methodologies.
5. identify the social and ethical issues relating to computer science enabling them to be
professionals in the industry.
7.3 Industry Demand
Computers play an ever-increasing role in the lives of people. The increased use of ICT in
every aspect of our lives ensures the continued relevance of this area of speciality. Furthermore,
companies  that  design  computer  systems  require  professionals  that  possess  software
development skills to complement traditional hardware skills.  Additionally, in line with the
government’s digital inclusion agenda that seeks to develop a robust framework to support the
digitalisation of the economy in a manner that captures and benefit every citizen, the BT ech in
Computer Science programme has been developed to produce graduates with the necessary
hardware and software skills as well as analytical prowess. Graduates from this programme are
ideally placed to pursue their specialisation in Computer Science, which has wide applicability
both nationally and internationally, within many indigenous technological companies and the
multinational sectors. More, with the exponential development of ICT technologies worldwide,
graduates will be at the forefront of people being employed in these areas.

8.2  Progression Requirements 
i. A student shall be deemed to have failed a course if he/she obtains a numerical
equivalent grade zero (0) or below the 50% mark. 
ii. A student referred in a course(s) shall take a re-sit examination(s).
iii. A student shall have only two chances at re-sit examination(s) in any course(s)
failed. 
iv. Where a student takes re-sit examinations his/her total credits earned for the
determination of GPA shall be increased by the credits assigned to the course(s).
v. A student who obtains pass grades in his entire course in a semester but does not
obtain a CGPA of at least 1.50 shall be deemed to have failed the programme. 
vi. A student shall be allowed to proceed to take the course(s) in the subsequent
semester on probation to redeem himself/herself by bringing the CGPA to at least
1.50.
vii. A student whose semestral GPA falls below 1.0 shall be withdrawn from the
programme. 
8.3  Graduation Requirements 
To be considered for the award of Degree, a candidate must obtain:
i. The minimum entry requirements for the programme of study.
ii. Followed an approved programme of study in the  University.
iii. Completed and passed all required courses.
iv. Taken courses to earn a minimum of 139 credit hours. 
v. Obtained a Final Cumulative Grade Point Average (FCGPA) of at least 1.50 in
each of the eight semesters. 
vi. Obtained a Grade Point Average (GPA) of at least 1.50 in each of the eight
semesters. 
vii. The classification of the award of Degree shall be determined by the FCGPA and

BCSC 101: PRINCIPLES OF PROGRAMMING
Course Description
This  course  is designed  to  be  an  introduction  to  the  fundamentals  of  computer  programming.
Students will be introduced to programming techniques for designing, writing and debugging simple
computer  programs.  It  will  enable  students  to  get  a  solid  grasp  of  the  basic  mechanics  of
programming leading to the subsequent acquisition of stronger programming skills. 
Course Objectives
After successful completion of this course, students will acquire the knowledge and skill to:
1. explain programming concepts and terminologies.
2. describe the distinguishing characteristics between the imperative and procedural 
programming language.
3. design algorithms and flowcharts to solve problems.
BTech. Computer Science (HTU) Page 10

===== PAGE 12 =====
4. choose an appropriate programming solution for a given task.
5. demonstrate basic programming skills to write simple programs.
Course   Outline  
 Introduction to programming Concepts and Terminologies: Low-level Languages (LLL),
High-Level Language (HLL), the role of a program to perform a task, Translators, etc.
 Programming tools and techniques: algorithms, pseudo-code, flowcharts, etc.
 Elements of programming language: Character set, constants, data types, variables, variable
declaration, operators, expressions.
 Program Flow Control: If statement, If…Else statement, Switch statement, while loop, Do…
while loop, for loop, etc.
 Basic concepts of object-oriented programming.
 Basics of identifying and fixing errors.
Mode of Delivery
Face-to-Face sessions, Workshops, Seminars and Lab sessions.

BCSC 103: DISCRETE MATHEMATICS
Course Description
Discrete mathematics is an aspect of mathematics devoted to logical reasoning and mathematical
deductions. It is characterized by mathematics involving countable sets. It serves as the bedrock for
computer programming.  Discrete mathematics deals with  the  study of mathematical structures.
Topics include set theory and binary operations, number systems, functions and relations, sequence
and series, induction and recursion, graph theory, logic, and Boolean algebra. 
BTech. Computer Science (HTU) Page 11

===== PAGE 13 =====
Course Objectives
At the end of the course, students should be able to:
1. explain and prove basic concepts in set theory; 
2. demonstrate knowledge in functions and their properties;
3. demonstrate knowledge and application in sequence and series;
4. apply mathematical induction and recursion to prove functions, sequence and series;
5. apply graph theory to operations research problems; and
6. evaluate and demonstrate knowledge in logics.
Course Outline
 Set Theory & Binary Operations
 Number Systems
 Functions and Relations
 Sequence and Series
 Induction and Recursion
 Graph Theory

BCSC 105: BASIC ELECTRONICS
Course Description
This  course  introduces  students  to  semiconductor  physics,  diodes  and  their  applications,  basic
transistors,  RLC  circuits  as  well  as  basic  digital  electronics.  This  course  also  explains  the
understanding  of  the  signals  in  the  time  domain  and  frequency  domain.  Students  are  able  to
understand various types of signals.
Course Objectives
Upon completion of this course, the student will be able to:
1. identify and describe the operation of semiconductor materials and devices used in analog 
circuits and some typical applications;
2. explain the operational principles and models of bipolar junction transistors (BJT);
3. classify various types of signals;
4. describe basic digital logic gates and digital logic families;
5. analyse basic RLC circuits.
Course Outline
 Introduction  to  Semiconductor  Physics:  Energy  bands  and  charge  carriers  in
semiconductors: energy bands - metals- semiconductors and insulators direct and indirect
semiconductors charge-carriers in semiconductors: electrons and holes-intrinsic and extrinsic
material: n-material and p-material-carrier concentration.
 Diode  theory  and  applications: Basic  idea  about  forward  bias,  reverse  bias  and  VI
characteristics, ideal diode, second and third approximation, surface mount diodes, Zener
diode, Testing of diode with multi-meter, half wave rectifier, full-wave rectifier, bridge
rectifier,  RC  and  LC  filters,  Design  of  un-regulated  and  regulated  DC  power  supplies,
Clipping  circuit,  Clamping  circuit,  voltage  multiplier  circuit,  Reading  datasheet  of
semiconductor diodes.
 Transistors: BJT operation, BJT voltages and currents, CE, CB and CC characteristics, DC
load line and bias point, base bias, emitter feedback bias, collector feedback bias, voltage
divider bias, Thermal stability, biasing BJT switching circuits, transistor power dissipation
and  switching  time,  Testing  of  bipolar  junction  transistor  with  multi-meter,  Reading
datasheet of BJT.

HOTU 100: AFRICAN STUDIES
 Course Description
This course introduces students to the field of African Studies including Africa’s histories, peoples
and cultures. The course is split into two parts namely the political aspect and socio-cultural aspect.
BTech. Computer Science (HTU) Page 15

===== PAGE 17 =====
Course Objectives
At the end of this course, students will be able to understand the role of Africa in world affairs from
three perspectives – the Rise of Africa, Africa’s Cultural Heritage and Africa in the Modern World.
Specifically, the course aims to: 
1. give general focus on how Africa’s past has shaped its present political and socio-economic
conditions. 
2. provide  students  with  the  opportunity  to  acquaint  themselves  with  the  African  cultural
heritage and a holistic view of the contemporary African scene.
Course Outline
Unit I:  The Political Section
The political section comprises the colonial rule in Africa, nationalism in Africa, party systems
and military rule in Africa, good governance, the problem of nationhood and national unity,
regional integration and the quest for unity, the debt problems in Africa and dependency in
Africa. 
Unit II: The Socio-Cultural Section
The socio-cultural section is made up of  the African cultural heritage such as socio-cultural
values,  political  organization-traditional  politics  and  government  in  Africa,  the  cultural
framework of economic activity, cultural change and continuity. The course also covers issues on
Africa’s  position  in  the  modern  world  such  as  post-independence  economic  development,
population and development in Africa and women and development.
Mode of Delivery
The modes of delivery are lectures and class discussions.

Course Objectives

1. write basic grammatical sentences in academic essays;
2. read and identify the main ideas in a text;
3. make academic presentations in class;

a. Regulations on Students Assessment 
The assessment of students shall cover the following areas. 
 Course  Assignments: They  shall  include  class exercises,  tests,  quizzes,  homework,
course papers (essays), practical exercises and student projects. The assignments shall
carry 20% of the overall marks for the course. There shall be at least one assignment for
a course.
 Mid-Semester Examinations: This accounts for 20% of the overall marks of the course.
It will be conducted by the course instructor/lecturer about mid-way through the semester
but mainly at his/her convenience using the regular period(s) on the teaching timetable.

 End-of-Semester Examinations: The last three weeks of the semester will be devoted to
