# Curriculum research: Data Analytics

Accessed 2026-10. University names appear only in this document. Figures marked "(not stated)" were not on the page that was retrieved; figures marked "(search summary)" come from a search-result summary because the page itself could not be fetched (PDF text unreadable, 403 or redirect); treat those as needing a check.

## 1. Summary

- "Data analytics" and "business analytics" are the usual names for the applied, decision-oriented sibling of data science. Bachelor's programmes (NUS Business Analytics, Pretoria Information and Knowledge Systems, Stellenbosch's analytics and optimisation focal area) mix statistics, programming, databases and visualisation with business, finance and information-systems courses and end in a capstone plus an internship or dissertation.
- Master's programmes are typically one year (Georgia Tech 36 credit hours, Warwick 1 year full-time, Wits Big Data Analytics Honours 1 year). They combine a computing course, a statistical/machine-learning modelling course, an operations research (optimisation) element, a business course and a practicum or dissertation.
- Doctoral study in analytics is rarely a "PhD in Data Analytics"; it sits inside statistics, operations research, information systems or business-analytics departments. Common features: 1 to 2 years of coursework, a qualifying exam after year 1 or 2, an applied data-analysis project or paper, a dissertation proposal in year 3 and a defence. The evidence collected for this level is thinner than for the other levels (see section 2.3).
- Compared with Data Science, analytics puts more weight on spreadsheets and business intelligence, dashboards, experimentation/causal reasoning, forecasting, optimisation and prescriptive decision models, and on domain subjects (accounting and finance, business processes, health), and less on deep learning and mathematical theory.
- The platform's current 12-course Data Analytics list covers the data-handling and BI core but lacks calculus and linear algebra, programming depth, regression, machine learning, optimisation, experiments, research methods and a capstone, and has no master's or PhD content.

## 3. Common core across universities

1. Programming for data (Python or R) and data structures (NUS, LSE, Berkeley, Georgia Tech computing course).
2. Calculus, linear algebra, probability and statistics (NUS first two years, LSE years 1 and 2, Berkeley lower division).
3. Databases, data management and visualisation (NUS, LSE, Georgia Tech data and visual analytics).
4. Statistical modelling and regression/econometrics (NUS econometrics modelling, LSE applied regression, Georgia Tech analytics modelling).
5. Machine learning and data mining.
6. Optimisation/operations research as at least one required element (Georgia Tech all tracks, Wits discrete optimisation, Berkeley depth list).
7. A business or application domain element (NUS business applications and IS leadership, Georgia Tech introduction to business, Warwick knowledge-transfer dissertations).
8. A capstone, practicum or industry project (NUS capstone and internship, Georgia Tech 6-credit practicum, Wits research project, CMU capstone).
9. Research methods before a dissertation (Warwick, Wits).

## 4. Prerequisite chains

- NUS: calculus and linear algebra, programming methodology, probability and statistics, then econometrics modelling and data management, then application systems development and electives (levels 3000 and 4000), then the capstone and the internship.
- Georgia Tech: the three introductory core courses (computing, business, analytics modelling) can be waived by an elective; data and visual analytics and data analytics in business are the advanced core; track electives; practicum alongside or after electives.
- Georgia Tech ISYE 6501 prerequisites: probability and statistics, basic programming, linear algebra, basic calculus; R is helpful but not required.
- Wits: honours coursework (research methods, machine learning, data analysis, visualisation, optimisation) then a research project; a BSc with second-year computer science and mathematics is required.
- Warwick: foundations modules in term 1, options, then a summer dissertation.
- Doctoral: coursework and qualifying exam, applied analysis project, proposal in year 3, dissertation.

### 5.3 Doctoral level

No public week-by-week doctoral analytics seminar was found. The closest documented structures are:
- CMU Advanced Data Analysis: year 1 spring begins the project; year 2 fall completes written and oral presentations with an outside investigator.
- CMU 10-715 (Fall 2025), a PhD-level core course in machine learning, see the Data Science research note for its schedule: foundations, learning theory, neural networks, bandits and reinforcement learning, causality, language models, generative models.
- Georgia Tech CS 7545 (Spring 2026) topic outline: concentration inequalities and convex optimisation; PAC learning; VC dimension and Rademacher complexity; online learning and bandits; assessed by assignments, two midterms, a final and a paper presentation.

## 6. Gap analysis against the current platform

Current Data Analytics list (12): spreadsheet-analytics, introduction-to-python, probability-and-statistics, database-systems-and-design, data-wrangling-and-preparation, statistical-inference, data-visualisation, business-intelligence-and-dashboards, data-mining-and-warehousing, time-series-analysis-and-forecasting, data-science, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing.

Present and well matched: data handling, visualisation, BI and dashboards, forecasting, inference, ethics.

Missing at bachelor's:
- Mathematics and programming base: `calculus`, `linear-algebra-for-data-science`, `data-structures-and-algorithms` (all in the catalogue).
- Business and domain: `business-process-modelling`, `accounting-and-finance-for-information-systems` (in catalogue); electives such as `enterprise-resource-planning`, `financial-mathematics`, `epidemiology-and-public-health-data`.
- Modelling: `machine-learning` (in catalogue), regression and generalised linear models, causal inference and experimental design, operations research and decision modelling (new).
- `research-methods-in-computer-science` (in catalogue) and a capstone project (new).

Missing at master's: advanced statistical modelling, advanced machine learning, optimisation and prescriptive analytics, graduate research methods, an applied practicum. Existing courses that fit as electives: `natural-language-processing`, `deep-learning`, `mlops-and-model-deployment`, `responsible-ai`, `cloud-computing`, `reinforcement-learning`, `financial-mathematics`, `business-intelligence-and-dashboards`, `big-data-and-parallel-computing`.

Missing at PhD: a doctoral research seminar, statistical learning theory, and milestones.
