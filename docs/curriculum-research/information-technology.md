# Information Technology: curriculum research

Accessed 2026-10. Research for the CS Virtual Lab programme `information-technology`. University names appear only in this document.

## Summary

- Bachelor level: IT degrees are practice-oriented four-year (US, Sri Lanka, South Africa) or three-year (UK) programmes built on a shared base: computing fundamentals, programming, networking, databases, web, system administration, information security, human-computer interaction, systems analysis and project management, finished with a capstone (senior design, group project, industrial training or co-op). Specialisation is by track (networking and systems, cybersecurity, software development, game development, data).
- Master's level: 30 to 39 US credits or 180 UK/African credits over 1 to 2 years. The typical shape is a core of 5 to 8 foundation and management courses, a concentration or optional pool, and a capstone, project or dissertation.
- PhD level: 72 US credits (reducible by up to 30 for a related master's), four breadth/qualifying examinations, a plan of study of about 18 credits in a concentration, an oral comprehensive exam, a dissertation proposal, and 24 dissertation credits.
- The existing 17-course IT programme is a solid practitioner spine (support, networking, sysadmin, DevOps, cloud, security, service management, project management) but lacks the mathematics and statistics foundation, programming depth, systems analysis, HCI, advanced web, scripting/automation, systems integration/enterprise architecture, and everything at master's and PhD level.

Limits of this research: the ACM/IEEE IT2017 report PDF could not be read in this session (binary), the St Andrews, Maseno and Universiti Utara Malaysia pages were not reachable, and the University of Pretoria yearbook returned only search snippets. Few African degree pages were readable (University of Malawi master's, University of Pretoria snippets). No public week-by-week syllabus was found for a master's IT core or a doctoral IT seminar; those entries below are marked and kept partial instead of being invented.

## Common core across universities

1. Computing fundamentals and programming: every bachelor reviewed (Cincinnati IT 1050, 1090C; Pretoria COS 110/INF 164).
2. Networking: Cincinnati computer networking and network infrastructure management; Edison telecommunications and networking.
3. Databases: Cincinnati database management I and II; Edison database design; Malawi database systems.
4. System administration: Cincinnati system administration; Epoka system administration course (Windows Server and UNIX/Linux).
5. Information security: Cincinnati information security and assurance; Malawi IT governance and security; Pace/Edison concentrations in cybersecurity.
6. Systems analysis and design and project management: Pretoria INF 370, Cincinnati IT 3020 and IT 4020, Malawi Project Management, Edison systems analysis and technology project management.
7. Research methods and dissertation or capstone at master's: Malawi Research Methodology and dissertation, Edison and Pace capstones, Saint Rose master's project.
8. Mathematics/statistics for IT at bachelor: Cincinnati pre-calculus, discrete mathematics, applied statistics.
9. HCI/user experience: Cincinnati IT 2021, IT2017 User Experience Design domain.
10. Capstone/work experience: Cincinnati co-op and senior design, Moratuwa industrial training.

## Prerequisite chains

- Fundamentals of IT -> computer networking -> network infrastructure management -> network security (Cincinnati track sequence).
- Programming I -> programming II -> web, mobile and enterprise application development (Cincinnati software track).
- Database management I -> database management II / applied business intelligence (Cincinnati).
- System administration -> cloud computing -> infrastructure and service operations (Cincinnati Networking/Systems track).
- Programming and algorithms -> software engineering -> project management -> dissertation (Malawi).
- Pretoria: INF 154 is the prerequisite for INF 164 programming; COS 110 is the base for later computer science modules.
- Master's core usually follows a foundations course where entrants lack a computing degree (Edison foundations course).

## Representative weekly schedules

### Bachelor core: Fundamentals of System Administration (Epoka University, CEN 304, 14 weekly topics, 5 ECTS)

1. Windows Server overview, features, installation
2. Active Directory, Group Policy Objects
3. User configuration, user profiles, disk quotas
4. Access control lists and permissions
5. DNS, DHCP, IIS
6. Backup and recovery
7. Introduction to UNIX and Linux
8. Midterm
9. Introduction to graphical environments
10. Startup and shutdown scripts (booting, kernel, init)
11. Software package administration
12. User and group administration
13. File permissions
14. Disk partitioning and mounting file systems

Assessment: homework 10%, midterm 30%, two quizzes 10%, final 40% (the page's weights sum to 90%). No prerequisite listed. Workload 125 hours.

### Master's core: Enterprise Architecture (partial, from search results only)

Carleton University's Sprott School outline (BUSI 3405, a business course whose level was not confirmed) lists: Week 1 strategic IS and enterprise architecture foundations; Week 2 digital strategy and project discussion; Week 3 architecture frameworks (TOGAF, Zachman, agile enterprise architecture), Assignment 1; Week 4 architecture development, tools and communication, Quiz 1. The rest of the schedule could not be read. Addis Ababa Science and Technology University (Spring 2026) lists enterprise architecture frameworks in week 2, then service-oriented architecture, methodology overview and agile architecture, with a midterm in week 8 (snippet). No complete public master's IT core calendar was found.

### Doctoral seminar (pattern only, partial)

Public doctoral seminars in information systems and IT follow a week-by-week sequence of research areas with papers read and presented by students (City University of New York, Baruch College CIS 86000: participation 20%, each paper judged on theoretical basis and research design), a foundations course ending in a journal-style research proposal (UNLV MIS 761: topic description, article-search method, reference list, presentation, proposal) and theory-building courses with mandatory pre-class readings (KIT, University of Hong Kong). A full, readable, week-by-week doctoral IT schedule was not available in this session.

## Proposed structure

### Bachelor of Science in Information Technology (4 years, 25 core + 10 elective pool, 5 electives required)

Core in study order: fundamentals-of-computer-science, principles-of-programming, introduction-to-python, computer-hardware, discrete-mathematics, probability-and-statistics, computer-networking-i, operating-systems, it-support-and-troubleshooting, database-systems-and-design, web-application-programming-i, systems-administration-i, computer-networking-ii, NEW Scripting and IT Automation, human-computer-interaction, systems-administration-ii, web-application-programming-ii, object-oriented-system-analysis-and-design, version-control-and-devops, cloud-computing, information-security, NEW Systems Integration and Enterprise Architecture, it-service-management-and-governance, it-project-management, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing.

Electives: containers-and-kubernetes, infrastructure-as-code, serverless-and-cloud-native-applications, cryptography-and-network-security, security-operations-and-incident-response, internet-of-things, mobile-application-development, business-intelligence-and-dashboards, enterprise-resource-planning, software-defined-networking.

Capstone (not a catalogue course): two-semester senior design project and optional co-op or industrial training.

### Master of Science in Information Technology (1 to 2 years, 6 core + 8 elective pool, 4 electives required)

Core: NEW Graduate Research Methods, NEW Advanced Computer Networks, NEW Advanced Operating Systems, NEW Enterprise Architecture and Digital Transformation, NEW Cloud Architecture and FinOps, distributed-systems.

Electives: site-reliability-engineering, software-defined-networking, infrastructure-as-code, containers-and-kubernetes, cloud-security, security-operations-and-incident-response, big-data-and-parallel-computing, regulatory-technology-and-compliance.

Milestones: research proposal, capstone project or dissertation, dissertation defence.

### Doctor of Philosophy in Information Technology (4 to 5 years, 3 core + 6 elective pool, 3 electives required)

Core: NEW Graduate Research Methods (shared), NEW Doctoral Research Seminar in Information Technology, NEW Advanced Operating Systems.

Electives: NEW Advanced Computer Networks, NEW Enterprise Architecture and Digital Transformation, NEW Cloud Architecture and FinOps, distributed-systems, big-data-and-parallel-computing, machine-learning.

Milestones: breadth/qualifying examinations (four written exams), plan of study in a concentration, oral comprehensive examination, dissertation proposal, teaching or research assistantship, at least one refereed publication, dissertation, public defence.
