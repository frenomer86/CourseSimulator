# Curriculum research: Computer Science

Accessed 2026-10. University names appear only in this document. Figures marked "(not stated)" were not on the page that was retrieved; figures marked "(third-party)" come from an aggregator and need checking against the university. The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through the research, so Cambridge's MPhil, ETH's master's structure, Stellenbosch and the Wits/Pretoria undergraduate lists could not be completed (see section 9).

## 1. Summary

- Every leading programme builds the same spine: programming (two or three courses), discrete maths and proof, calculus, linear algebra, probability/statistics, data structures and algorithms, a separate algorithms-design/analysis course, computer organisation, operating systems, a theory-of-computation course, and (in most) databases, networks, software engineering and an AI/ML course. Linear algebra is present in every programme checked and is missing from the platform's current 53-course list.
- Bachelor's size: Stanford 96 units in the major (43 in CS core, depth and senior project); MIT about 177 to 183 units in the major; NUS 160 units (80 programme units); ETH 180 ECTS in 3 years; Oxford 3 years (BA) or 4 years (MCompSci). The platform's 53 courses are already larger than any single student pathway, so they must become "core plus electives".
- Depth is chosen late: Stanford has 8 depth tracks, NUS 10 focus areas (3 primary courses each), Oxford has 21 options, ETH 3 specialisations.
- Capstone/project: Stanford senior project (at least 3 units), NUS industry experience (6 to 12 units) or dissertation, Oxford group design practical in year 2 and optional individual project in years 3 and 4. The platform has no capstone.
- Master's: 45 units at Stanford (foundations, breadth, depth, a significant implementation); 90 ECTS with a 6-month thesis at ETH; Cape Town 90 coursework credits (Research Methods plus six electives) and a minor dissertation; NUS MComp 52 units (24 core, 16 electives, 12 capstone). Research methods is compulsory in the African programmes checked.
- PhD: course work is small and breadth-driven (Stanford 3 breadth courses from different areas, NUS 24 units across three clusters, Cape Town thesis only plus a methods course). The shared milestones are a qualifying examination (Stanford end of year 3, NUS within 18 months), a thesis proposal (Stanford and NUS by end of year 4), teaching, and a public defence.
- Constraint for this programme: the current bachelor list holds all 53 original courses and every one is kept. The proposal re-labels 29 of them as electives (24 stay core) and adds 5 core slots (linear algebra, functional programming, algorithm design, concurrency, capstone).

## 5. Common core across universities

Present in at least four of the programmes checked (MIT, Stanford, Oxford, NUS, ETH, UCT):

1. Programming fundamentals in two or three courses (MIT 6.100/6.1010/6.1020, Stanford CS106A/B, Oxford Functional + Imperative, NUS Programming Methodology I/II, ETH Introduction to Programming, UCT CSC1015F/CSC1016S).
2. Discrete mathematics and proof (MIT 6.1200, Stanford CS103, Oxford Discrete Mathematics and Introduction to Proof Systems, NUS Discrete Structures, ETH Diskrete Mathematik).
3. Linear algebra, calculus and probability (all six).
4. Data structures plus a dedicated algorithm design and analysis course (MIT 6.1210 and 6.1220/6.1400, Stanford CS161, Oxford, NUS CS2040S and CS3230, ETH).
5. Computer organisation / digital systems (MIT 6.1910, Oxford Digital Systems, NUS CS2100, ETH Digital Design and Computer Architecture).
6. Operating systems and systems engineering (MIT 6.1800/6.1810, Stanford CS107/110, NUS CS2106, ETH).
7. Theory of computation / models of computation (MIT 6.1400, Oxford Models of Computation).
8. A software engineering course with team project (MIT 6.1020 Software Construction, NUS CS2103T).
9. An introductory AI and machine learning course (NUS CS2109S, MIT AI requirement of 12 units).
10. A final-year project or industry experience (Stanford, NUS, Oxford).

## 6. Prerequisite chains

Chains stated on public pages:

- UCT: CSC1015F (needs 70% in school mathematics) leads to CSC1016S; both lead to CSC2001F (also needs first-year university mathematics); CSC2001F leads to CSC2002S; CSC2001F + CSC2002S lead to CSC3002F and CSC3003S; CSC2001F leads to CSC2003S (Computer Games), then CSC3020H. Extended route CSC1010H, CSC1011H.
- Oxford: Core 1 (year 1) is the prerequisite for Core 2 plus options (year 2); options in year 3; advanced options in year 4 need a 2:1.
- NUS: foundation courses (discrete structures, programming methodology, data structures and algorithms) feed the 4000-level focus-area courses; MA1301 bridging for students without H2 maths (individual prerequisite codes not retrieved).
- Stanford MS: foundations (CS103, probability, CS161, CS107, CS110) are prerequisites unless waived.

Proposed platform chains (study order):

- Programming: principles-of-programming → introduction-to-python / programming-with-c → data-structures-and-algorithms → Design and Analysis of Algorithms → Advanced Algorithms (master) → Advanced Topics in Theoretical Computer Science (PhD).
- Maths: discrete-mathematics + calculus → linear-algebra-for-data-science → mathematics-for-computer-science → probability-and-statistics → theory-of-computation.
- Systems: digital-logic-design → computer-organization-and-architecture → operating-systems → distributed-systems → Advanced Operating Systems (master); computer-networking-i → distributed-systems.
- Software: software-development-with-java → software-engineering → Concurrent and Parallel Programming; compiler-construction → Programming Language Theory and Verification (master).
- Learning: probability-and-statistics + linear-algebra → artificial-intelligence → machine-learning (elective) → Advanced Machine Learning (master).
- Research: research-methods-in-computer-science → Computing Capstone Project (bachelor) → Graduate Research Methods (master) → Doctoral Research Seminar (PhD).

## 9. Proposed structure

New bachelor courses (4): Functional Programming; Design and Analysis of Algorithms; Concurrent and Parallel Programming; Computing Capstone Project. New master's courses (7): Graduate Research Methods; Advanced Algorithms; Advanced Operating Systems; Advanced Machine Learning; Programming Language Theory and Verification; Advanced Database Systems; Advanced Computer Architecture. New PhD courses (2): Doctoral Research Seminar in Computer Science; Advanced Topics in Theoretical Computer Science.

### Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (4 years)

- Core (29, in study order): fundamentals-of-computer-science, principles-of-programming, introduction-to-python, discrete-mathematics, calculus, linear-algebra-for-data-science, mathematics-for-computer-science, probability-and-statistics, programming-with-c, digital-logic-design, data-structures-and-algorithms, Functional Programming (new), software-development-with-java, computer-organization-and-architecture, database-systems-and-design, operating-systems, computer-networking-i, software-engineering, Design and Analysis of Algorithms (new), theory-of-computation, Concurrent and Parallel Programming (new), artificial-intelligence, information-security, compiler-construction, distributed-systems, human-computer-interaction, research-methods-in-computer-science, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing, Computing Capstone Project (new).
- Electives (29), choose 8: basic-electronics, computer-hardware, programming-with-cpp, web-application-programming-i, web-application-programming-ii, assembly-language-and-microprocessors, data-communications, computer-networking-ii, systems-administration-i, systems-administration-ii, version-control-and-devops, information-systems-and-e-commerce, numerical-methods-and-computation, computer-graphics, embedded-systems, it-project-management, object-oriented-system-analysis-and-design, advanced-software-development-with-java, mobile-application-development, machine-learning, data-science, data-mining-and-warehousing, big-data-and-parallel-computing, cloud-computing, internet-of-things, wireless-communication, cryptography-and-network-security, computer-forensics, blockchain-and-distributed-ledgers.
- A student takes 29 core plus 8 electives = 37 courses (inside the 26 to 40 range). The pool listed in the proposal is 58 because all 53 originals are kept (53 + linear-algebra-for-data-science + 4 new = 58), which exceeds the brief's 40-course cap on the list; this follows the instruction to keep all 53.

### Master of Science in Computer Science (1 to 2 years)

- Core (5): Graduate Research Methods, Advanced Algorithms, Advanced Operating Systems, Advanced Machine Learning, Programming Language Theory and Verification.
- Electives (11), choose 5: Advanced Database Systems, Advanced Computer Architecture, distributed-systems, cryptography-and-network-security, deep-learning, natural-language-processing, computer-vision, reinforcement-learning, knowledge-representation-and-reasoning, compiler-construction, computer-graphics.
- Milestones: research proposal in year 1; breadth across theory, systems and learning; dissertation or capstone; oral defence.

### Doctor of Philosophy in Computer Science (4 to 5 years)

- Core (2): Doctoral Research Seminar in Computer Science, Graduate Research Methods.
- Electives (7), choose 3 from different areas: Advanced Algorithms, Advanced Operating Systems, Advanced Machine Learning, Programming Language Theory and Verification, Advanced Topics in Theoretical Computer Science, Advanced Database Systems, Advanced Computer Architecture.
- Milestones: first-year rotations and advisor selection; breadth requirement; qualifying examination (assigned-paper review and presentation, two attempts); teaching requirement; thesis proposal and reading committee by end of year 4; publications; dissertation; public defence plus closed oral.
