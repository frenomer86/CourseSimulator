# Game Development: curriculum research

Programme slug: `game-development`. Research accessed 2026-10. University names appear only in this document.

## Summary

- Game-development degrees fall into two shapes. Computer-science-first degrees (Utah CS with the games emphasis, USC BS Computer Science (Games), Abertay, DigiPen, Cape Town) keep a full computing core and add games on top. Games-first degrees (Utah BS Games, Wits Digital Arts) put design and art first and carry less maths and systems. The platform serves CS-first learners, so this proposal follows the first shape.
- Every CS-first degree has the same spine: two programming courses, C++, data structures, algorithms, discrete mathematics, calculus, linear algebra, probability, computer organisation, an operating-systems or systems course, software engineering, graphics, a game engine course, game AI, game physics, and a team or capstone project lasting one to two terms.
- The current platform programme has 13 courses. It already holds the games-specific four (design, engine, physics, graphics) plus AI, HCI, software engineering and C++. It is missing the maths spine (discrete, calculus, probability), computer organisation, operating systems, algorithms, a game AI course, game networking and a capstone.
- At master's level the programmes are 90 to 120 ECTS or about 32 to 40 US units. They combine a technical backbone (algorithms, AI, graphics, engine engineering), a studio or project sequence, electives, and either a thesis (ITU 30 ECTS, Utrecht 40 EC research) or a project sequence with no thesis (USC).
- At PhD level there is no standard taught structure. Cape Town and Malta treat the PhD as a research degree. UC Santa Cruz Computational Media is the one public example with a defined course and exam ladder.
- Proposal: bachelor 21 core plus 15 electives (36 courses, 4 new); master's 6 core plus 9 electives (15, 7 new); PhD 3 core plus 6 electives (9, 2 new).

## Common core across universities

Appears in at least four of the bachelor programmes above:

1. Introductory programming (two-course sequence) and C or C++.
2. Data structures and algorithms, plus a separate algorithm analysis course in 3 of 5 (Utah, USC, DigiPen).
3. Calculus, linear algebra, discrete mathematics, probability.
4. Computer organisation and an operating-systems or systems course.
5. Computer graphics and a game engine course.
6. Game design and a production or studio course.
7. Game AI (separate course at Abertay through Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, and at ITU, Hawaii, Northeastern, UCSC).
8. A team game project each year, ending in a senior capstone.

At master's level the shared pieces are: an algorithms or AI foundation (USC), advanced graphics (USC, ITU, Utrecht), engine or high-performance programming (Utah, ITU), a studio project sequence (USC, Utah, ITU), a research module (ITU, Utrecht) and a thesis or research project (ITU, Utrecht, Malta).

## Prerequisite chains

- Programming: Introductory programming → C++ → data structures → algorithms → engine programming.
- Mathematics: Calculus → linear algebra → computer graphics → game physics. Discrete maths feeds algorithms.
- Graphics: Linear algebra + C++ → computer graphics → real-time and shader work (ITU graphics programming, Abertay shaders).
- AI: Probability + data structures → artificial intelligence → game AI → player modelling and content generation.
- Systems: Computer organisation → operating systems → engine architecture (memory, threads) → high-performance game programming.
- Project: Game design → studio or capstone, sequenced across years (DigiPen, Abertay, USC).
- Doctoral: A master's or first-class bachelor's (Malta), CMPM 201 before 202 and 203 (UCSC).

### Master's core: Advanced Artificial Intelligence and Games (University of Malta, 10 ECTS)

Master's level (EQF 7); prerequisites a bachelor's in computing or engineering plus object-oriented programming. Lecture, tutorial and project. The public page gives a topic outline, not a dated week list.

1. Revisiting game AI and non-traditional uses of AI in games.
2. Player modelling: inputs, outputs and approaches; game analytics and data mining; affective computing.
3. Procedural content generation: constructive, search-based and machine-learning approaches.
4. Representing generated content.
5. Evaluating generators and visualising their output.
6. Applications combining player models and generators.

Assessment: 10-minute presentation 10%, 20-minute oral examination 40%, report 50%.

Public weekly schedules for the other master's cores (Utah Game Engineering I to III, ITU Game Programming, USC 3D Graphics and Rendering) were not retrievable; Utah's three-course engine sequence is listed in the master's table.

### Doctoral seminar format: paper-reading seminar (Georgia State, CSc 8821) and UCSC methods courses

No public doctoral game-research syllabus with dated weeks was found; the search budget ran out before more could be tried. Two public references describe the format:

- Georgia State CSc 8821 (topic order, no dates): genetic algorithms and neuroevolution, Monte Carlo tree search, finite state machines, procedural content generation, pathfinding, agents, reinforcement learning. Students read, present and review research papers; assessment is participation 5%, midterm 20%, assignments about 40%, project 35% with proposal, mid-semester update and final presentation.
- UCSC CMPM 202 (research strategies and case studies of past projects) and CMPM 203 (methods: playtesting, arts critique, ethnographic observation, surveys, data mining, user experiments) are the catalogue descriptions; weekly content is not public.

4. game-design-principles

11. human-computer-interaction

15. operating-systems
16. game-engine-programming
17. game-physics-and-simulation
18. artificial-intelligence
19. NEW Game AI
20. NEW Multiplayer and Network Game Programming
21. NEW Computing Capstone Project

Electives: introduction-to-python, database-systems-and-design, computer-networking-i, theory-of-computation, machine-learning, mobile-application-development, software-architecture-and-design-patterns, software-testing-and-quality-assurance, version-control-and-devops, assembly-language-and-microprocessors, numerical-methods-and-computation, data-visualisation, web-application-programming-i, embedded-systems, research-methods-in-computer-science.

### Master of Science in Game Development (1 to 2 years, 6 core + 9 electives, 4 electives required)

Core: Advanced Artificial Intelligence, Graduate Research Methods, Advanced Computer Graphics, Advanced Game Engine Architecture, Procedural Content Generation and Player Modelling, Graduate Game Project (all new).

Electives: Character Animation and Crowd Simulation (new), machine-learning, deep-learning, reinforcement-learning, computer-vision, distributed-systems, knowledge-representation-and-reasoning, optimisation-for-machine-learning, responsible-ai.

Milestones: research proposal approved before the thesis semester; playable prototype with technical report and portfolio review; master's thesis or directed research project with oral defence.

### Doctor of Philosophy in Game Technology and Interactive Media (4 to 5 years, 3 core + 6 electives, 3 electives required)

Core: Doctoral Research Seminar in Game Technology and Interactive Media (new), Empirical Research Methods for Interactive Systems (new), Graduate Research Methods (shared; waived if already taken at master's).

Electives: Procedural Content Generation and Player Modelling, Advanced Computer Graphics, Character Animation and Crowd Simulation, reinforcement-learning, deep-learning, computer-vision. The master's courses in the pool are for entrants from other fields; entrants from this programme choose the others.

Milestones: supervisor and topic agreed before admission; first-year literature synthesis review; qualifying examination (literature review plus portfolio or prototype, written and oral); dissertation proposal defence with an external committee member; teaching practicum; at least one peer-reviewed publication; dissertation with built artefact or empirical study, and oral defence.

## Doubts and limits

- The ITU curriculum PDFs and USC catalogue pages could not be fetched (connection refused, certificate rejected). ITU details come from search summaries of the 2026 curriculum and programme page; the technology-track module list with ECTS was not confirmed.
- Cape Town material comes from a 2014 flyer and a search summary of the careers page; a current handbook was not reached. The Cape Town BSc games courses are described as three semester courses, not a named degree.
- Malta's taught-unit list was not retrieved. Utah BS Games credits come from search summaries.
- DigiPen credit totals are the Singapore campus figure; the Redmond page gave no credits.
- The USC "Computer Science (Games)" bachelor page returned a current requirement list; its title and the master's page title are as shown there.
- Abertay's year-by-year placement of modules is not public on the pages reached.
- Weekly schedules for master's and doctoral courses are partial (see above). No African doctoral games syllabus was found; Stellenbosch, Pretoria, Makerere, Nairobi and KNUST gave no game-specific degree in the searches run. The WebSearch budget ran out before this could be extended.
