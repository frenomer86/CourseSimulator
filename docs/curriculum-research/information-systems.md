# Information Systems: curriculum research

Research date: October 2026. Source pages were read through search and fetch tools; where a page could not be fetched
(server error or unreadable PDF) the figure is marked "from search summary" and should be re-checked before it is quoted.

## Summary

Information Systems (IS) sits between computer science and business. Every programme studied has the same spine:
a small programming and data foundation, a systems analysis and design sequence, databases, an enterprise or
business-process layer (ERP, process modelling, architecture), a management layer (projects, service management,
governance, security, ethics), and a capstone that is a real or simulated client project. Maths content is lighter than
in computer science (statistics and discrete maths or calculus), and accounting or economics appears in most degrees.

The current platform programme (18 courses) already covers the spine well. It is missing: an economics course, an
enterprise-architecture and integration course, a digital-transformation/innovation course, and a capstone consulting
project. There is no master's or PhD layer at all. The proposal adds 4 bachelor, 6 master's and 2 PhD courses.

- All three finish with a client or team project (CMU consulting project, NUS capstone, UCT systems development project).
- Programming is compulsory but modest: one or two courses (intro programming, data structures or commercial programming).

Observations
- Common sequence everywhere: foundation and methods coursework, qualifying exam or qualifying paper, proposal defence, dissertation defence, with teaching in the middle years.
- Methods training is split into qualitative and quantitative strands plus a theory and research-design foundation course.

## Common core across universities

1. Programming and data structures (1 to 2 courses).
2. Databases.
3. Systems analysis and design, requirements, process modelling.
4. Enterprise systems, ERP, IT architecture.
5. Statistics (and some calculus or discrete maths).
6. Accounting, finance or economics for business context.
7. HCI or user-centred design.
8. IT project management, service management and governance.
9. Information security, ethics and law.
10. Digital transformation and innovation (CMU, LSE, NUS).
11. Capstone client project.

## Prerequisite chains

- Introductory IS or computing -> programming -> data structures -> database -> enterprise systems architecture -> enterprise server and interface development -> capstone.
- Systems analysis -> systems design and development -> systems development project (UCT); Application Design and Development must come before the innovation-and-entrepreneurship breadth course (CMU).
- Statistics -> business intelligence and analytics -> data science electives.
- Master's: research design is taken before the dissertation; PhD: foundation courses before the qualifying exam, then proposal.

## Gap analysis against the current platform

Present (18): fundamentals-of-computer-science, introduction-to-python, spreadsheet-analytics, database-systems-and-design, business-process-modelling, accounting-and-finance-for-information-systems, information-systems-and-e-commerce, object-oriented-system-analysis-and-design, web-application-programming-i, computer-networking-i, human-computer-interaction, enterprise-resource-planning, business-intelligence-and-dashboards, it-project-management, it-service-management-and-governance, information-security, research-methods-in-computer-science, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing.

Missing from the bachelor: requirements-engineering and software-engineering (exist in the catalogue, not in this programme), data-structures-and-algorithms (every degree has one), discrete-mathematics and statistics (probability-and-statistics), economics (new), enterprise architecture and integration (new), digital transformation and innovation (new), capstone (new).

Missing entirely: master's layer (research design, IS theory, IT strategy, advanced analysis and modelling, analytics, data governance) and PhD layer (research seminar, advanced research design, IS theory).

## Proposed structure

### Bachelor of Science in Information Systems (4 years, 27 core + 12 elective pool, choose 6)
Core in study order:
fundamentals-of-computer-science, introduction-to-python, spreadsheet-analytics, discrete-mathematics, probability-and-statistics, data-structures-and-algorithms, new: Economics for Technology and Business, accounting-and-finance-for-information-systems, database-systems-and-design, business-process-modelling, information-systems-and-e-commerce, object-oriented-system-analysis-and-design, requirements-engineering, web-application-programming-i, computer-networking-i, human-computer-interaction, enterprise-resource-planning, new: Enterprise Architecture and Integration, software-engineering, business-intelligence-and-dashboards, it-project-management, it-service-management-and-governance, information-security, new: Digital Transformation and Innovation Management, research-methods-in-computer-science, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing, new: Information Systems Capstone Project.

Electives (choose 6): data-wrangling-and-preparation, data-visualisation, data-science, data-mining-and-warehousing, statistical-inference, web-application-programming-ii, mobile-application-development, cloud-computing, software-architecture-and-design-patterns, blockchain-and-distributed-ledgers, digital-payments-and-mobile-money, regulatory-technology-and-compliance.

### Master of Science in Information Systems (1 to 2 years, 6 core + 9 elective pool, choose 4)
Core: new: Graduate Research Methods, new: Information Systems Theory and Digital Innovation, new: Strategic Management of Information Technology, new: Advanced Systems Analysis and Modelling, new: Advanced Business Analytics, new: Data Governance and Information Management.
Electives: it-service-management-and-governance, software-architecture-and-design-patterns, data-mining-and-warehousing, big-data-and-parallel-computing, machine-learning, cloud-computing, information-security, regulatory-technology-and-compliance, responsible-ai.
Milestones: research proposal, industry consulting project or placement, master's dissertation (about 8,000 to 15,000 words), final examination or viva.

### Doctor of Philosophy in Information Systems (4 to 5 years, 4 core + 5 elective pool, choose 3)
Core: new: Doctoral Research Seminar in Information Systems, new: Advanced Research Design for Information Systems, new: Graduate Research Methods, new: Information Systems Theory and Digital Innovation.
Electives: statistical-inference, new: Advanced Business Analytics, machine-learning, responsible-ai, new: Data Governance and Information Management.
Milestones: qualifying examination (graduate research paper) within 18 months, thesis proposal by end of year 3, teaching duties in years 2 to 3, pre-submission seminar, dissertation and defence.
