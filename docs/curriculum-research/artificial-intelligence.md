# Curriculum research: Artificial Intelligence

Accessed 2026-10. University names appear only in this document. "(not stated)" means the retrieved page did not give the figure; "(third-party)" means an aggregator source.

## 1. Summary

- Artificial Intelligence exists as a named bachelor's degree at CMU (BS in AI), Edinburgh (BSc AI, and AI with Computer Science) and NUS (BComp in AI). At Stanford it is a track inside the CS degree.
- Bachelor's level: about 30 subject courses. Four blocks of maths (calculus, linear algebra, probability, discrete maths), a programming/algorithms/systems block, a three to five course AI core (search, reasoning and agents, ML, NLP or vision, ethics), AI electives (robotics, planning, speech, knowledge graphs), and an honours project or internship.
- Master's level: usually 180 credits (Edinburgh, Stellenbosch) or 1 year of coursework plus 1 year of research (Wits, UCT). A typical structure is 4 to 6 compulsory courses (machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning, vision, research methods), 3 to 6 electives, and a 60-credit dissertation.
- PhD level: CMU, Stanford and Edinburgh's doctoral training centres combine coursework (0 to 7 courses), a qualifying or depth exam, a proposal and a defence; South African PhDs are research-only with a research master's as entry.
- The current platform's 14-course AI list has the right AI core (AI, knowledge representation, ML, DL, NLP, vision, RL, responsible AI) but misses algorithm analysis, systems, multivariable calculus, a capstone, cognitive science and robotics/planning options, and it has no master's or PhD content.

## 3. Common core across universities

1. Maths: calculus, linear algebra, probability/statistics, discrete maths (all four bachelor's programmes).
2. Programming, data structures, algorithm design and software engineering (CMU, Edinburgh, NUS).
3. Computer systems/organisation (CMU, Edinburgh, Stanford).
4. AI foundations: search, games, logic and reasoning, planning, agents (Stanford, Edinburgh "reasoning and agents", NUS).
5. Machine learning and deep learning, plus NLP or vision (CMU, NUS, Edinburgh, Wits, Stellenbosch).
6. Responsible AI / professional ethics (NUS core, CMU ethics requirement, Edinburgh professional issues).
7. Robotics and decision-making options (Edinburgh, NUS, Wits).
8. Cognitive science options (CMU requirement in cognitive science or psychology, Edinburgh computational cognitive science).
9. Project work: honours project (Edinburgh 40 credits), internship (NUS), senior project (Stanford), research report (Wits, UCT).
10. Master's: ML, deep learning, reinforcement learning, vision, probabilistic modelling, research methods (Wits, Stellenbosch, UCT, Edinburgh).

## 4. Prerequisite chains

- Programming (two courses) and discrete maths to data structures to algorithm analysis to AI (Stanford AI principles course requires programming I and II, discrete mathematics, probability and linear algebra).
- Calculus and linear algebra to optimisation to machine learning to deep learning to NLP/vision/RL.
- Probability and statistics to statistical inference to probabilistic modelling (Edinburgh third/fourth year, Stellenbosch master's).
- Logic and discrete maths to knowledge representation and reasoning, theory of computation.
- Pretoria AI 314 requires the first-year programming course.
- Master's: ML plus probability to probabilistic graphical models and advanced ML; PhD core draws on the master's core.

Prerequisites: programming I and II, discrete maths, probability, linear algebra (recommended: algorithms and systems).

Berkeley CS188 structure for comparison: uninformed search, A*, CSPs (week 2), game trees and expectimax (week 3), MDPs (week 4), RL (week 5), probability and Bayes nets (weeks 6-8), HMMs and particle filtering (weeks 9-10), machine learning (weeks 10-12), guest lectures on AI policy and a final review. The page lists no prerequisites.

### 5.3 Doctoral seminar (Harvard foundations of deep learning, Spring 2021 weekly list)

Weeks: classical learning theory and over-parameterised models; learning dynamics and simplicity bias; unsupervised and contrastive learning; robustness, out-of-distribution and poisoning; variational inference and statistical physics; NLP; interpretability and theoretical neuroscience; causality and fairness; bandits and reinforcement learning; privacy. Format: reading before each lecture, experimental homework reproducing a paper, a project, possible scribe notes. Prerequisites: proof fluency, probability and information theory, theory of computation and algorithms, undergraduate ML, Python.

## 6. Gap analysis against the current platform

Current AI list (14): introduction-to-python, discrete-mathematics, linear-algebra-for-data-science, probability-and-statistics, data-structures-and-algorithms, theory-of-computation, artificial-intelligence, knowledge-representation-and-reasoning, machine-learning, deep-learning, natural-language-processing, computer-vision, reinforcement-learning, responsible-ai.

Bachelor's gaps:
- Programming foundation (principles-of-programming exists but is not in the list); calculus; software engineering; computer organisation; databases (all offered at all four bachelor's benchmarks).
- Multivariable calculus and an algorithm analysis course (missing from the platform entirely; NUS, CMU, Stanford).
- Optimisation (exists as optimisation-for-machine-learning; NUS makes it core).
- Cognitive science (CMU, Edinburgh; missing).
- Robotics and control options (exist: mobile-robotics-and-navigation, robot-programming-with-ros, control-systems).
- Capstone/honours project (missing).

Master's gaps: advanced ML, graphical models, advanced deep learning, advanced AI (planning, search, reasoning), multi-agent systems and game theory, computational intelligence (evolutionary and swarm methods; UCT, Wits, Edinburgh), research methods.

PhD gaps: planning and decision-making under uncertainty, doctoral research seminar.

## 8. Caveats

- Stanford, ETH-style and some African pages were reachable only in part; Stanford MS duration, Wits credit values and Edinburgh's full-time MSc length were not on the pages retrieved.
- No public weekly syllabus for an AI-specific master's core was retrieved; the graphical-models course stands in. The doctoral seminar is a machine learning theory seminar.
- Makerere and Nairobi curricula could not be retrieved.
- The proposed PhD course "Planning and Decision Making Under Uncertainty" is supported by NUS's planning elective, CMU's decision-making-under-uncertainty menu course and graduate AI courses, but no single doctoral syllabus was retrieved.
- The AI programme reuses Advanced Machine Learning, Probabilistic Graphical Models, Advanced Deep Learning, Graduate Research Methods, Multivariable Calculus, Design and Analysis of Algorithms and Computing Capstone Project, which the Machine Learning proposal also defines; titles are identical so they can be merged.
