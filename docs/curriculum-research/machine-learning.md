# Curriculum research: Machine Learning

Accessed 2026-10. University names appear only in this document. Figures marked "(not stated)" were not on the page that was retrieved; figures marked "(third-party)" come from an aggregator and need checking against the university.

## 1. Summary

- Machine learning is rarely a stand-alone bachelor's degree. It is taught as an AI track or concentration inside a computer science degree (Stanford, NUS, Edinburgh, CMU's BS in AI), and as a dedicated master's and PhD (CMU's Machine Learning Department, Stellenbosch).
- Bachelor's level: about 30 to 33 subject courses after general education, built on four maths blocks (calculus, linear algebra, probability/statistics, discrete maths), four programming/algorithms blocks, then 3 to 6 AI/ML courses and a capstone or honours project.
- Master's level: 6 core courses (introductory ML, deep learning, probabilistic graphical models, applied ML, optimisation, statistics) plus 3 electives plus a practicum or a thesis. One-year programmes dominate (CMU 16 months, Stellenbosch and Wits 1 year, Edinburgh 12 months in 180 credits).
- PhD level: 4 required core courses plus 2 menu courses plus 1 elective, then research. Milestones are a speaking requirement, teaching (two semesters), a first-author paper or data-analysis project, a qualifying/depth exam, a thesis proposal and a defence.
- The current platform's 17-course Machine Learning list is a good bachelor's skeleton but is missing multivariable calculus, algorithm analysis, regression/GLMs and a capstone at bachelor's level, and has no master's or PhD content.

## 3. Common core across universities

1. Mathematics: calculus, linear algebra, probability, statistics, discrete maths (every bachelor's reviewed; NUS and CMU also require optimisation or regression).
2. Programming and algorithms: two programming courses, data structures and a dedicated algorithm design course (NUS, CMU, Stanford).
3. Introductory AI and introductory ML as separate courses (all).
4. Deep learning, plus NLP or vision (CMU BSAI requires one; NUS requires one; Stanford requires two of four AI areas).
5. Responsible/ethical AI (NUS core course, CMU ethics requirement, Edinburgh professional issues).
6. A capstone, industry experience or honours project (Stanford senior project, Edinburgh honours, NUS internship/dissertation).
7. At master's level: probabilistic graphical models, optimisation, mathematical statistics, deep learning, research methods (CMU, Stellenbosch, Wits, UCT).
8. At PhD level: advanced ML theory, statistics, research seminar, speaking and teaching requirements.

## 4. Prerequisite chains

- Python, calculus, linear algebra, probability to introductory ML to deep learning to NLP / vision / RL (Stanford AI course prerequisites: two programming courses, discrete maths, probability, linear algebra; Pretoria COS 314 requires COS 110).
- Probability and statistics to statistical inference to regression/GLM to advanced statistics (CMU 36-218, 36-401, 36-705).
- Data structures to algorithm design to advanced algorithms/large-scale ML.
- Introductory ML and optimisation to probabilistic graphical models and advanced ML at master's level (MIT-style graduate ML courses assume undergraduate ML plus linear algebra, probability, algorithms).
- Master's core to PhD core (CMU PhD core overlaps the master's core).

## 6. Gap analysis against the current platform

Current Machine Learning list (17): introduction-to-python, calculus, linear-algebra-for-data-science, probability-and-statistics, data-structures-and-algorithms, optimisation-for-machine-learning, statistical-inference, data-wrangling-and-preparation, machine-learning, artificial-intelligence, deep-learning, natural-language-processing, computer-vision, reinforcement-learning, big-data-and-parallel-computing, mlops-and-model-deployment, responsible-ai.

Already well covered: the AI/ML teaching core (machine-learning, deep-learning, NLP, vision, RL, responsible AI, MLOps) and optimisation.

Missing at bachelor's level:
- Multivariable calculus (taught at Stanford, CMU, NUS before ML).
- A dedicated algorithm analysis course (NUS, CMU, Stanford).
- Regression and generalised linear models (CMU requires modern regression; CMU master's offers regression analysis as an option).
- A capstone (Stanford senior project, Edinburgh honours project, NUS dissertation).
- Supporting courses already on the platform but not in the list: discrete-mathematics, numerical-methods-and-computation, database-systems-and-design, data-science, time-series-analysis-and-forecasting.

Missing at master's level (everything): advanced ML, probabilistic graphical models, advanced deep learning, mathematical statistics, convex/large-scale optimisation, generative AI/LLMs, graduate research methods.

Missing at PhD level: statistical learning theory, doctoral research seminar.

Milestones: speaking requirement, two semesters of teaching, first-author paper (writing requirement), qualifying/depth exam, thesis proposal, dissertation and defence with an external examiner.

## 8. Caveats

- CMU does not offer a BS in Machine Learning; the bachelor's benchmark uses its BS in AI and the Stanford/NUS/Edinburgh AI tracks.
- Stanford, ETH and several African pages were reachable only in part; credits for Wits modules, Stanford MS duration and the Edinburgh full-time MS duration were not stated on the pages retrieved.
- Makerere and Nairobi curricula could not be retrieved; Africa evidence comes from Stellenbosch, Wits, Cape Town, Pretoria and CMU Africa.
- The weekly schedule for the doctoral theory course comes from an undated course page.
