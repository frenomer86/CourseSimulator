# Health Informatics: degree-structure research

Research notes for the CS Virtual Lab programme `health-informatics`. University names appear only in these notes. Sources were read in October 2026. The evidence base is strongest for the US (Oregon Health & Science University, Pittsburgh, UC San Diego), the accreditation body's published competencies (CAHIIM/AMIA) and two Asian/UK-linked programmes (Zhejiang-Edinburgh, National Taiwan University). The web-search budget ran out part-way through, so no verified taught programme page from Africa or continental Europe was reached; that is flagged in section 10.

## 1. Summary

- Health informatics sits between health care, information science and social/behavioural science. The US accreditation body (CAHIIM) lists baccalaureate competencies in ten foundational domains (health; information science and technology; social and behavioural science; health information science and technology; human factors and socio-technical systems; social and behavioural aspects of health; applied informatics; professionalism; interprofessional practice; leadership) and accredits master's programmes. The master's standards are the AMIA 2017 core competencies.
- Master's programmes are professional and often part-time/online: Oregon's MS is 49 credits (non-thesis, 43 coursework plus a 6-credit capstone or internship) or 55 credits (thesis), about two years full-time; Pittsburgh's MS is 36 credits over two years with a research project and a first-author article.
- PhD programmes are research degrees of 4 to 5 years: Pittsburgh 72 credits (29 core, 12 elective, 3 teaching, 18 dissertation), Oregon 135 credits with a cognate area, UC San Diego 16 units of core plus 16 of electives. All have a first-year or second-year examination, a dissertation proposal/candidacy stage and teaching.
- The platform has 14 health informatics courses at bachelor level. It lacks clinical science foundations, healthcare delivery/quality, clinical decision support, a capstone, and the whole master's and PhD layers (foundations, statistics, machine learning for healthcare, evaluation and implementation, clinical research informatics, research methods, seminar, proposal writing).

## 2. Bachelor's level

Bachelor's degrees named "health informatics" are far less standardised than master's degrees. CAHIIM states that standards for the baccalaureate are still under development and publishes a 2023 competency framework instead; programme-level data is therefore thinner here.

Reading across: the bachelor's content that all sources agree on is programming, databases, data analysis, human-computer interaction, systems analysis, standards/interoperability, security, ethics and a first course on how health care is delivered. Clinical science (anatomy, physiology, terminology) is the one area where bachelor's programmes need content that is absent from a pure computing curriculum.

## 5. Common core across universities

1. Introduction to biomedical and health informatics: definitions, history, scope, resources (Oregon, Pittsburgh foundations 1 and 2, UC San Diego principles, Zhejiang-Edinburgh introduction 1 to 3).
2. Health records, clinical information systems and clinical workflow (Oregon, CAHIIM, Pittsburgh).
3. Standards, terminologies and interoperability (Oregon, CAHIIM).
4. Clinical decision support, evidence-based medicine, information retrieval (Oregon, MIT medical decision support).
5. Programming, databases, data analysis (every programme).
6. Biostatistics, epidemiology and research methods (Oregon evaluative sciences, Pittsburgh statistics, CAHIIM).
7. Machine learning/AI for biomedical data (Pittsburgh core, Oregon, MIT).
8. Human factors and usability (CAHIIM F5, Oregon HCI).
9. Privacy, security, ethics and law (Oregon ethics, CAHIIM F8).
10. Project management, leadership and organisational behaviour (Oregon, CAHIIM F10).
11. Evaluation and implementation of health IT (Oregon design and evaluation, Pittsburgh, CAHIIM).
12. Capstone, internship or research project (Oregon, Pittsburgh, Zhejiang-Edinburgh).
13. Seminar, journal club, colloquium and responsible conduct of research at PhD level (Pittsburgh, UC San Diego, Oregon).

## 6. Prerequisite chains

- Programming: introduction to programming then databases then health information systems then standards/interoperability and clinical research data.
- Statistics: probability and statistics then biostatistics then epidemiology then evaluation and research methods (Oregon quantitative research methods); at master's level statistical foundations then machine learning for healthcare (MIT requires an undergraduate machine learning course).
- Health foundation: clinical terminology and physiology then electronic health records then clinical decision support and health information exchange.
- Oregon: introduction to biomedical informatics has no prerequisite; machine learning for healthcare requires the machine learning course; capstone requires a signed project agreement and adviser.
- PhD: first-year core and rotations, then preliminary examination (Pittsburgh), then comprehensive/qualifying examination (year 2), then proposal and candidacy (years 2 to 3), then dissertation.

## 7. Representative weekly schedules

### 7.1 Bachelor-level introductory core: Oregon "Introduction to Biomedical and Health Informatics" (10x10 offering)

Public outline of the 10-unit survey course adapted from the Oregon graduate course (each unit about 4 to 8 hours; two weeks of posted material then a third week to finish):

| Unit | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Overview and motivating problems: definitions and history of the field and of AI; problems in health care; who does informatics; resources |
| 2 | Computing concepts: types of computers, data storage, hardware and software, networks, software engineering |
| 3 | Electronic and personal health records: clinical data, history, examples, data entry, clinical decision support, personal health records |
| 4 | Standards and interoperability: identifier and transaction standards, message exchange, terminologies, SMART on FHIR |
| 5 | Artificial intelligence: data science, machine learning, predictive AI, generative AI, trustworthy AI |
| 6 | Advanced use of the EHR: patient safety, quality measurement, health information exchange, population health, NLP, from meaningful use to interoperability |
| 7 | Implementation, security and evaluation: workflow redesign, system selection, telehealth, privacy and security, evaluation |
| 8 | Information retrieval: knowledge-based information, content, indexing, retrieval, evaluation |
| 9 | Research informatics: clinical research informatics, molecular biology primer, translational bioinformatics, precision medicine, omics data in the EHR |
| 10 | Other areas: nursing, consumer health, public health and imaging informatics, evidence-based medicine |

The course project is a 2 to 3 page proposal of an informatics solution to a local problem. This is a good scaffold for a bachelor's introductory course because it needs no prior clinical knowledge.

### 7.2 Master's core: MIT Machine Learning for Healthcare (graduate, spring 2019, two 1.5-hour sessions per week)

Prerequisite: undergraduate machine learning. Grading: homework 40, projects 40, participation (scribing, community consulting) 20.

1. Introduction: what makes healthcare unique.
2. Overview of clinical care.
3. Deep dive into clinical data.
4 and 5. Risk stratification, parts 1 and 2.
6. Physiological time series.
7 and 8. Natural language processing, parts 1 and 2.
9. Translating technology into the clinic.
10. Machine learning for cardiology.
11. Machine learning for differential diagnosis.
12. Machine learning for pathology.
13. Machine learning for mammography.
14 and 15. Causal inference, parts 1 and 2.
16. Reinforcement learning.
17. Evaluating dynamic treatment strategies.
18 and 19. Disease progression and subtyping, parts 1 and 2.
20. Precision medicine.
21. Automating clinical workflows.
22. Regulation of ML/AI (US) and human subjects research.
23. Fairness.
24. Robustness to dataset shift.
25. Interpretability.

### 7.3 Doctoral seminar format

No public week-by-week doctoral seminar syllabus was found. Documented formats: Oregon's doctoral symposium (3 credits, state-of-the-art literature synthesis in a research area, student symposia scheduled over several weeks, each presentation at most 20 minutes, students questioned and graded); Pittsburgh's weekly colloquium (guest speakers, graded on participation) and weekly journal club (students present classical and recent papers and critique peers); UC San Diego's colloquium in each quarter of years 1 and 2.

Suggested weekly pattern for the platform seminar: week 1 how to read and critique an informatics paper; weeks 2 to 6 student-led discussions of clinical AI, interoperability and evaluation papers; weeks 7 to 9 reproduce a published analysis or critique a deployment case study (scenario engine); weeks 10 to 12 mock comprehensive examination (20 minute talk, panel questions); weeks 13 to 14 writing a manuscript section; final week presentations.

## 8. Gap analysis against the current platform

Current programme (14 courses): fundamentals-of-computer-science, introduction-to-python, database-systems-and-design, biostatistics, health-information-systems, health-data-standards-and-interoperability, epidemiology-and-public-health-data, electronic-health-records, human-computer-interaction, data-science, information-security, digital-health-and-telemedicine, it-project-management, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing.

| Gap | Evidence | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Only 14 courses; peers are larger | CAHIIM framework, Zhejiang-Edinburgh | Add programming, data, systems analysis and business-process courses from existing slugs |
| No clinical science | CAHIIM health domain F1, Zhejiang-Edinburgh | New: Human Anatomy, Physiology and Clinical Terminology |
| No healthcare delivery, quality and safety | Oregon, CAHIIM F1 | New: Healthcare Delivery, Quality and Patient Safety |
| No decision support / evidence-based practice | Oregon, MIT | New: Clinical Decision Support and Evidence-Based Practice |
| No capstone | Oregon, Zhejiang-Edinburgh | New: Computing Capstone Project (shared) |
| No master's or PhD layer | Oregon, Pittsburgh, UC San Diego | New master's courses below |
| Africa-first angle: district health information systems (DHIS2) deployment | University of Oslo HISP, which develops DHIS2 used across Africa | Cover in Healthcare Delivery (low-resource settings) and Evaluation and Implementation of Health Information Systems; consider a later DHIS2-based lab scenario (E5, E10) |

Engine fit: E5 (SQL, ER, star schema for clinical data and standards), E10 (branching case studies with evidence and rubrics for implementation, ethics, decision support), E9 (BPMN workflow redesign, UML), E11 (phone emulator for mHealth/telehealth), E1 (Python for data and ML), E7 (statistics and Monte Carlo for epidemiology). Hospital-network, device and imaging integration (HL7 message flows, DICOM) can reuse E3 and E5 but have no dedicated engine.

## 9. Proposed structure

### Bachelor of Science in Health Informatics (4 years, 26 core plus 12 elective pool, 6 electives required)

Core in suggested study order:
fundamentals-of-computer-science, principles-of-programming, introduction-to-python, **Human Anatomy, Physiology and Clinical Terminology (new)**, **Healthcare Delivery, Quality and Patient Safety (new)**, probability-and-statistics, spreadsheet-analytics, database-systems-and-design, health-information-systems, biostatistics, epidemiology-and-public-health-data, electronic-health-records, health-data-standards-and-interoperability, data-wrangling-and-preparation, data-visualisation, human-computer-interaction, business-process-modelling, object-oriented-system-analysis-and-design, information-security, digital-health-and-telemedicine, **Clinical Decision Support and Evidence-Based Practice (new)**, data-science, it-project-management, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing, research-methods-in-computer-science, **Computing Capstone Project (new, shared)**.

Electives: business-intelligence-and-dashboards, data-mining-and-warehousing, machine-learning, mobile-application-development, web-application-programming-i, requirements-engineering, software-engineering, it-service-management-and-governance, cloud-computing, molecular-biology-for-computing, time-series-analysis-and-forecasting, regulatory-technology-and-compliance.

### Master of Science in Health Informatics (1 to 2 years, 6 core plus 9 elective pool, 4 electives required)

Core: Foundations of Biomedical and Health Informatics (new), Advanced Statistical Modelling (new, shared), Machine Learning for Healthcare (new), Evaluation and Implementation of Health Information Systems (new), Clinical Research Informatics and Real-World Data (new), Graduate Research Methods (new, shared).
Electives: Advanced Machine Learning (new, shared), natural-language-processing, responsible-ai, deep-learning, cloud-computing, secure-software-development, software-architecture-and-design-patterns, regulatory-technology-and-compliance, business-intelligence-and-dashboards.
Milestones: project proposal approved by adviser; applied capstone or internship (non-thesis) or two-semester thesis; written report or first-author article; presentation to a master's committee; responsible conduct training.

### Doctor of Philosophy in Health Informatics (4 to 5 years, 5 core plus 5 elective pool, 3 electives required)

Core: Doctoral Research Seminar in Health Informatics (new), Research Proposal and Grant Writing (new, shared), Advanced Statistical Modelling, Evaluation and Implementation of Health Information Systems, Machine Learning for Healthcare (courses already taken at master's level are replaced by electives).
Electives: Advanced Machine Learning, natural-language-processing, responsible-ai, deep-learning, Clinical Research Informatics and Real-World Data.
Milestones: rotations and adviser choice (year 1); preliminary examination (end of year 1); comprehensive or qualifying examination (by end of year 2); cognate-area study; mentored teaching; ethics training; dissertation proposal and candidacy (years 2 to 3); doctoral symposium; dissertation and defence (year 4 to 5).

New courses: 4 bachelor, 7 master's, 2 PhD (the limits). Details are in `proposals/health-informatics.json` in the session notes folder.

## 10. Doubtful points

- The bachelor's level rests on the CAHIIM/AMIA competency framework plus one Asian/UK programme; no Africa-based or European bachelor's page could be retrieved. Several candidate programmes (Makerere, Manchester, Edinburgh, Cape Town, Wits) were looked for but their pages were not reachable or the web-search budget ran out. Treat Africa and Europe as unverified and revisit before the proposal is final.
- Oregon and Pittsburgh figures were read directly from the university pages and the Pittsburgh 2025-2026 handbook; UC San Diego from the catalogue; NTU from the institute's regulations; MIT from OpenCourseWare.
- The Oregon "10x10" outline is a professional introductory course, not a credit-bearing bachelor's course. It is used because it is the most detailed public unit-by-unit outline.
- "Health informatics" and "biomedical informatics" are used interchangeably by Oregon, Pittsburgh and UC San Diego. Where the platform splits bioinformatics and health informatics, shared master's courses (statistics, machine learning, research methods) deliberately overlap with the bioinformatics proposal.
