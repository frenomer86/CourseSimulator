# Networking and Telecommunications: curriculum research

Accessed 2026-10. Research for the CS Virtual Lab programme `networking-and-telecommunications`. University names appear only in this document.

## Summary

- Bachelor level: telecommunications degrees are engineering degrees. A three-year UK degree (Essex: 24 modules, 360 credits) or a four-year European/African one (Warsaw, Makerere, KNUST) combines maths, electronics, programming, signals and systems and electromagnetics in years 1 and 2 with networks, DSP, RF/optical/wireless systems, security and an individual project in years 3 and 4. Computer networking sits alongside, not above, the electronics and signals content.
- Master's level: 30 US credits (NJIT: 15 core, 6 specialisation, 9 elective; Boston University: 40 units, six core courses and four electives, now closed to new students), 180 UK credits (UCL, one year), or about 2 years in Ghana/Denmark (13 modules, KNUST/GCTU collaboration). The compulsory spine is advanced networks, digital/wireless communications, network performance and security, with a project, thesis or capstone option. Georgia Tech reaches networking through a CS specialisation (Computer Networks in the computing-systems core, network security and internetworking electives).
- PhD level: US programmes use area qualifying exams, coursework (Illinois: 96 hours from a bachelor's, 72 or 64 with a master's; Georgia Tech: intro course, four breadth courses, 6-hour minor), a proposal and a defence. The Ghana/Denmark PhD lists nine core coursework areas, including algorithmic aspects of telecommunication networks, random processes and network security. Cape Town examines by thesis alone after a research methods course.
- The current platform list (15 courses) has the applied spine (data communications, networking I and II, wireless, telecommunication systems, mobile and cellular, SDN, cryptography and network security) but lacks: most bachelor foundations (programming, probability, circuits, digital logic, DSP, computer architecture, operating systems), electromagnetics and antennas, optical communications, information theory and coding, a capstone, and the whole graduate and doctoral level (advanced networks, digital communications, wireless at graduate depth, performance modelling, network planning and regulation, research methods, doctoral seminar).

## Prerequisite chains

- Calculus and mathematics for computer science, then probability and statistics, then signals-and-systems, then digital-signal-processing, then wireless and mobile systems.
- basic-electronics then circuit-analysis then digital-logic-design then computer-organization-and-architecture, then embedded or VLSI-style electives.
- Electromagnetics, then antennas/RF, then wireless and satellite systems; electromagnetics also underpins optical communications.
- principles-of-programming then programming-with-c, with operating-systems after computer organisation.
- computer-networking-i then data-communications then computer-networking-ii then software-defined-networking.
- telecommunication-systems then wireless-communication then mobile-and-cellular-networks.
- Cryptography needs mathematics and networking I.
- Master's: MIT requires an undergraduate systems or networks course and elementary probability; Cape Town's networks course requires working knowledge of computer networks.
- Doctoral: research methods, two advanced courses, qualifier, proposal, defence.

## Proposed structure

### Bachelor (4 years)

Core, in suggested study order (27): calculus, mathematics-for-computer-science, probability-and-statistics, principles-of-programming, programming-with-c, introduction-to-python, basic-electronics, circuit-analysis, digital-logic-design, signals-and-systems, computer-organization-and-architecture, operating-systems, computer-networking-i, data-communications, new Electromagnetics, Antennas and RF Engineering, computer-networking-ii, digital-signal-processing, new Information Theory and Coding, telecommunication-systems, wireless-communication, mobile-and-cellular-networks, new Optical Communications and Fibre Networks, cryptography-and-network-security, systems-administration-i, systems-administration-ii, software-defined-networking, new Telecommunications Engineering Project.

That is 27 core courses: 23 existing and 4 new.

Electives (pool of 12, choose 6): internet-of-things, distributed-systems, cloud-computing, embedded-systems, real-time-systems, control-systems, sensors-and-actuators, version-control-and-devops, information-security, ethical-hacking-and-penetration-testing, linear-algebra-for-data-science, machine-learning.

### Master's (1 to 2 years)

Core (5): new Graduate Research Methods, new Advanced Computer Networks, new Advanced Digital Communications, new Advanced Wireless and Mobile Systems, new Performance Modelling and Queueing Systems.

Electives (pool of 10, choose 4): new Datacentre Networks and Network Virtualisation, new Telecommunications Network Planning and Regulation, software-defined-networking, cryptography-and-network-security, internet-of-things, cloud-computing, cloud-security, digital-signal-processing, distributed-systems, security-operations-and-incident-response.

Milestones: research proposal in year one, project or minor dissertation, oral defence. New master's courses: 7.

### PhD (4 to 5 years)

Core (3): new Doctoral Research Seminar in Networking and Communications, new Advanced Computer Networks, new Performance Modelling and Queueing Systems.

Electives (pool of 7, choose 3): new Advanced Digital Communications, new Advanced Wireless and Mobile Systems, new Datacentre Networks and Network Virtualisation, software-defined-networking, cryptography-and-network-security, machine-learning, optimisation-for-machine-learning.

Milestones: first-year proposal; qualifying examination (area reading list and assigned papers); dissertation proposal and preliminary examination; one teaching term; at least one refereed publication; dissertation and public defence. New PhD courses: 1.
