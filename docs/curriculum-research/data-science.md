## 1. Summary

- Data science is taught in three shapes: a stand-alone bachelor's (Berkeley BA, LSE BSc, Stellenbosch BDatSci, UCC BSc), a joint computing-plus-statistics degree, and a taught master's of 1 to 2 years that ends in a dissertation or capstone (Edinburgh, Wits, UCT, CMU). The doctorate is usually "PhD in Data Science" (NYU) or sits in a statistics or machine learning department (CMU).
- Bachelor's level: about 28 to 35 subject courses. A four-block base (programming and data structures, calculus and linear algebra, probability and statistics, databases) comes first. Then regression, machine learning, data engineering/visualisation, ethics, a modelling or "depth" choice and a final project. Berkeley requires two calculus courses plus linear algebra plus Data 8/Data 100 plus at least 8 upper-division courses (28 units). LSE spreads 12 units over three years with programming, statistics, maths, algorithms, databases, machine learning and an applied project.
- Master's level: a statistical-foundations course, a machine learning course, a data/computing course, an ethics or privacy course, research methods, and a dissertation of roughly one third to one half of the credits (Edinburgh 60 of 180; UCT 60 or 90 of 180; Wits one year of coursework then a research report; CMU 144 units with a team capstone).
- PhD level: NYU requires 72 credits (5 core courses, 39 elective credits, 18 credits of research rotations), a first-year comprehensive evaluation, a depth qualifying exam by the fourth semester, a teaching requirement and a dissertation defence with an external member. CMU's machine learning PhD uses 6 core courses plus 1 elective, then teaching, speaking and a thesis.
- The platform's current 17-course Data Science list is a sound bachelor's skeleton but lacks regression, multivariable calculus, discrete maths, causal inference/experiments, an AI course, research methods and a capstone, and has no master's or PhD content.

## 3. Common core across universities

1. Programming and data structures in Python or R (Berkeley CS 61A/61B, LSE programming, NUS programming and data structures, Stellenbosch introductory computer science).
2. Calculus and linear algebra, with a second calculus course at Berkeley and a "further mathematical methods" course at LSE.
3. Probability and statistical inference as a sequence, followed by regression/linear modelling (Berkeley, LSE, CMU, NUS).
4. Data handling: databases/SQL, data management and visualisation (LSE, Berkeley Data 100, NUS, UCT, Wits).
5. Machine learning as a required course at bachelor's (Berkeley, LSE year 3) and a graduate machine learning course at master's and PhD (CMU, NYU, Wits).
6. Ethics and human context: Berkeley's human contexts requirement, Wits data privacy and ethics, Edinburgh data ethics.
7. Research training and a final project: LSE applied statistics project, Wits research methods and research report, Edinburgh project proposal and dissertation, CMU capstone, NYU research rotations.

## 4. Prerequisite chains

- Berkeley: programming (CS 61A) and Data 8 (or Stat 20) lead to data structures, to Data 100 (upper-division core), to a modelling course (machine learning or decision-making) and then the depth and domain-emphasis courses. Calculus I and II and linear algebra run in parallel. (Data 100 prerequisites are not on the retrieved page; the lower-division list above is the major's own sequencing.)
- LSE: year 1 statistics, maths methods, programming/visualisation lead to year 2 probability and regression, linear algebra, algorithms and databases, then year 3 machine learning and AI with the applied project.
- Wits: honours-level coursework (data analysis, machine learning, visualisation, research methods, optimisation) feeds the MSc, and the MSc forbids repeating honours modules (more than 30 credits of overlap makes an applicant ineligible).
- CMU 10-715: linear algebra, probability, multivariable calculus and Python are prerequisites; machine learning itself is not, but the course is graduate only.
- NYU: core courses by semester 3, then comprehensive evaluation (semester 2), depth qualifying exam (semester 4), proposal (year 3), defence.
- Edinburgh: the compulsory course may be taken in any year but before the dissertation; the dissertation also needs a preparation course taken with the final taught credits.

Projects: two housing-price modelling projects and two spam/ham classification projects; a graduate-level project with proposal, EDA, progress report, peer review and final report. ### 5.2 Master's core: Advanced Introduction to Machine Learning (CMU, Fall 2025, graduate only)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| Foundations | Introduction; perceptrons, linear hypothesis classes, support vector machines; kernel methods and multi-layer perceptrons; recitation on optimisation, regression, logistic regression |
| History | Two lectures on the history of machine learning |
| Learning theory | Four lectures on learning theory; recitations on tail bounds, maximum likelihood and MAP |
| Neural networks | Representation power, CNNs, ResNets; model selection, bias-complexity trade-off, interpolation regime; midterm |
| Bandits, reinforcement learning, causality | Multi-armed bandits, reinforcement learning (3 lectures), graphical models and causality (2 lectures) |
| Language models | Attention and transformers (about 2 weeks) |
| Generative models | Diffusion models and GANs (about 3 weeks); final exam |

Prerequisites: linear algebra (SVD), probability (multivariate Gaussians, Bayes), multivariable calculus, Python. Textbook for part of the course: a standard learning-theory text.

### 5.3 Doctoral seminar: Machine Learning Theory (Georgia Tech, Spring 2026, topic outline; dated weeks not retrieved)

1. Mathematical preliminaries, concentration inequalities, convex optimisation.
2. PAC learning and generalisation bounds.
3. Uniform convergence, VC dimension, Rademacher complexity.
4. Online learning, multi-armed bandits, reinforcement learning.
Assessment: assignments 40 percent, two midterms 30 percent, final 20 percent, a research-paper presentation 10 percent. A graduate course from another university (search summary) places PAC learnability in weeks 1 and 2 and Rademacher complexity in weeks 9 and 10, with several weeks run as seminar sessions.

## 6. Gap analysis against the current platform

Current Data Science list (17): introduction-to-python, calculus, linear-algebra-for-data-science, probability-and-statistics, data-structures-and-algorithms, database-systems-and-design, data-wrangling-and-preparation, statistical-inference, data-visualisation, numerical-methods-and-computation, data-science, machine-learning, data-mining-and-warehousing, time-series-analysis-and-forecasting, big-data-and-parallel-computing, deep-learning, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing.

Present and well matched: the programming, statistics, data handling, machine learning and big-data spine.

Missing at bachelor's:
- Programming foundations beyond Python (existing `principles-of-programming`), discrete mathematics (existing `discrete-mathematics`), and optimisation (existing `optimisation-for-machine-learning`) are in the catalogue but not in the list.
- A second calculus course (Multivariable Calculus), regression and generalised linear models, causal inference and experimental design, an AI course (existing `artificial-intelligence`), research methods (existing `research-methods-in-computer-science`) and a capstone project.

Missing at master's: mathematical statistics, advanced machine learning, probabilistic graphical models, advanced optimisation, graduate research methods and a research/dissertation slot. Existing courses that fit as electives: `deep-learning`, `natural-language-processing`, `computer-vision`, `reinforcement-learning`, `mlops-and-model-deployment`, `cloud-computing`, `distributed-systems`, `responsible-ai`, `big-data-and-parallel-computing`.

Missing at PhD: statistical learning theory, a doctoral research seminar, and all milestones.
