# Financial Technology: curriculum research

Research date: October 2026. Source pages were read through search and fetch tools. Where a page could not be fetched
(server error, certificate error or unreadable PDF) the figure is marked "from search summary" and should be re-checked
before it is quoted.

## Summary

Financial technology (fintech) is a young degree area. Dedicated bachelor's degrees are mostly new (UK, US, Gulf, Asia),
master's degrees are well established (UK, Singapore, South Africa), and doctoral training is usually a finance or
information-systems PhD with a fintech thesis topic; only a few fintech-named PhDs exist (Hong Kong). The common
shape is: finance and economics foundation, programming and data, payments and banking technology, blockchain and
digital assets, AI and machine learning in finance, regulation and risk, and a capstone or dissertation.

The current platform programme (15 courses) has the technology side (Python, databases, web, mobile, cryptography,
blockchain, ML, security), plus payments, financial mathematics, accounting and regtech. It lacks: economics,
investments and portfolio theory, banking/lending/insurance technology, a capstone, the maths foundations (calculus,
linear algebra, statistics) that quantitative fintech programmes assume, and the whole master's and PhD layer
(econometrics, asset pricing and derivatives, risk management, DeFi, algorithmic trading, advanced ML).
The proposal adds 4 bachelor, 7 master's and 2 PhD courses.

Observations
- UK degrees are fully prescribed with all-core module lists; US degrees are majors inside a business school with a business core; the Singapore degree treats fintech as a specialisation of IS.
- Every degree has: Python or programming, a finance foundation (accounting, markets, investments), data analytics or ML, blockchain, and regulation or governance.
- Maths content differs: UK and US degrees stay light (statistics, econometrics); the master's degrees assume calculus and statistics.

Observations
- All master's degrees combine three blocks: finance and quantitative methods (econometrics, asset pricing, derivatives, risk), computing and data (ML, big data, software), and distributed ledgers/digital assets.
- The two African degrees are the most data-science oriented (statistical computing, supervised and unsupervised learning) and both require a research project; the Witwatersrand degree makes a quantitative-research-techniques course a prerequisite for machine learning.
- Every degree ends with a project or dissertation (3,000 words at the lightest, 25,000 words at the heaviest).

Observations
- The only fintech-named PhD requires both a technology and a finance qualifying examination, which shows the intended breadth.
- Finance PhD templates front-load a quantitative core (10 to 16 courses) in the first two years, then a research paper, then the dissertation.

## Common core across universities

1. Programming for finance (Python) and data analytics.
2. Accounting, corporate finance and investments.
3. Payments and digital banking.
4. Blockchain and digital assets (every degree, bachelor to master's).
5. AI, machine learning and big data in finance.
6. Regulation, compliance and ethics.
7. Econometrics and quantitative methods (master's).
8. Capstone, project or research report.

## Prerequisite chains

- Python -> programming and data analytics for finance -> AI and big data in finance (Sunderland).
- Python for financial applications is a prerequisite of the master's fintech course (Stevens).
- Quantitative research techniques -> machine learning (Witwatersrand).
- Blockchain -> advanced blockchain and digital assets (Sunderland level 4 to level 6).
- Foundations of finance -> investments -> financial modelling -> advanced financial analytics (Quinnipiac).
- PhD: qualifying exam (technology and finance parts) -> survey paper -> thesis.

## Gap analysis against the current platform

Present (15): introduction-to-python, financial-mathematics, accounting-and-finance-for-information-systems, database-systems-and-design, web-application-programming-i, information-systems-and-e-commerce, digital-payments-and-mobile-money, data-science, mobile-application-development, cryptography-and-network-security, information-security, blockchain-and-distributed-ledgers, machine-learning, regulatory-technology-and-compliance, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing.

Missing at bachelor level: calculus, discrete maths, probability-and-statistics, statistical-inference and time-series-analysis-and-forecasting (all exist in the catalogue); data structures; economics (new); investments and portfolio management (new); banking, lending and insurance technology (new); a capstone (new).

Missing entirely: econometrics and quantitative methods, asset pricing and derivatives, financial risk management, algorithmic and systematic trading, DeFi and digital assets (advanced), advanced machine learning, research methods, doctoral seminar, market and mechanism design. Financial software engineering (a core at one African master's) is not proposed as a separate course; the existing software-engineering and Python courses can carry it.

## Proposed structure

### Bachelor of Science in Financial Technology (4 years, 26 core + 12 elective pool, choose 5)
Core in study order:
introduction-to-python, calculus, discrete-mathematics, probability-and-statistics, linear-algebra-for-data-science, data-structures-and-algorithms, database-systems-and-design, new: Economics for Technology and Business, accounting-and-finance-for-information-systems, financial-mathematics, statistical-inference, web-application-programming-i, information-systems-and-e-commerce, digital-payments-and-mobile-money, new: Investments and Portfolio Management, new: Digital Banking, Lending and Insurance Technology, information-security, cryptography-and-network-security, blockchain-and-distributed-ledgers, data-wrangling-and-preparation, data-science, machine-learning, time-series-analysis-and-forecasting, regulatory-technology-and-compliance, legal-and-ethical-issues-in-computing, new: Financial Technology Capstone Project.

Electives (choose 5): mobile-application-development, web-application-programming-ii, cloud-computing, big-data-and-parallel-computing, deep-learning, natural-language-processing, data-visualisation, business-intelligence-and-dashboards, enterprise-resource-planning, software-engineering, secure-software-development, optimisation-for-machine-learning.

### Master of Science in Financial Technology (1 to 2 years, 6 core + 9 elective pool, choose 4)
Core: new: Graduate Research Methods, new: Financial Econometrics and Quantitative Methods, new: Asset Pricing and Derivatives, new: Advanced Machine Learning, new: Decentralised Finance and Digital Assets, new: Financial Risk Management.
Electives: new: Algorithmic and Systematic Trading, big-data-and-parallel-computing, deep-learning, natural-language-processing, reinforcement-learning, optimisation-for-machine-learning, cloud-security, secure-software-development, responsible-ai.
Milestones: research or applied project proposal, industry placement or fintech start-up project (optional), master's dissertation or research report, oral defence.

### Doctor of Philosophy in Financial Technology (3 to 5 years, 4 core + 6 elective pool, choose 3)
Core: new: Doctoral Research Seminar in Financial Technology, new: Graduate Research Methods, new: Financial Econometrics and Quantitative Methods, new: Advanced Machine Learning.
Electives: new: Mechanism Design and Market Microstructure, new: Decentralised Finance and Digital Assets, new: Asset Pricing and Derivatives, reinforcement-learning, optimisation-for-machine-learning, cryptography-and-network-security.
Milestones: qualifying examination in two parts (technology and finance) within 22 months, critical survey paper with 45-minute presentation, thesis proposal, teaching in two terms, publishable papers, dissertation and defence.
