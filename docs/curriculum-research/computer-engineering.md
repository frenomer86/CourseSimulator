# Computer Engineering: curriculum research

Accessed 2026-10. Evidence levels used below: **[page]** = read from the official page or PDF in this session; **[snippet]** = taken from a search-result summary only (not opened); **[3rd]** = third-party guide; **[general]** = widely published practice, not re-checked in this session. Where the web could not be read (several `cmu.edu` pages failed with certificate errors, the search budget ran out) the gap is stated, not filled.

## 1. Summary

Computer Engineering (CompE) sits between electrical engineering and computer science. Every programme studied has the same spine:

1. Maths and science block (calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, probability, discrete maths, physics).
2. Circuits and electronics (circuit analysis, analogue devices, digital logic), then signals and systems.
3. Programming and software (C/C++, data structures and algorithms, software engineering, operating systems).
4. The "hardware/software boundary" stack: computer organisation, instruction sets and assembly, microprocessors and embedded systems, computer architecture, real-time systems, networks.
5. A design thread that runs through every year (labs, design projects) and ends in a capstone or research project (8 to 40 credits).
6. A short list of application tracks: machine learning, security, communications, control, VLSI/FPGA.

The current platform list for CompE has 18 courses and covers items 2 to 4 reasonably well. It is missing analogue electronics and devices, electromagnetics, an HDL/FPGA design course, a capstone, probability/linear algebra/discrete maths as explicit core, data structures, software engineering, security and machine learning (all of which appear in two or more of the programmes below), and has no master's or PhD layer.

Master's programmes are 1 to 2 years (45 units at Stanford, 120 ECTS over 2 years at ETH) built from a depth area, a breadth requirement, technical electives and an optional or required thesis. PhDs are research degrees with a qualifying exam in the first 3 to 4 semesters, a dissertation proposal, annual review and a public defence. Doctoral coursework is light and heavily seminar-based.

## 5. Common core across universities

Appears in at least four of the six bachelor programmes (MIT, UIUC, Imperial, NUS, UCT, Wits):
- Calculus, linear algebra, differential equations/engineering maths (all six).
- Probability/statistics (UIUC ECE 313, Wits Probabilistic Systems, Imperial Mathematics for Engineers, NUS data literacy).
- Discrete maths (UIUC CS 173, NUS CS1231, Imperial Discrete Mathematics, MIT 6.1200).
- Programming (C/C++/Python) and data structures and algorithms (UIUC CS 225, NUS CS2040C, Wits DSA, MIT 6.1210).
- Circuit analysis and analogue electronics (UIUC ECE 110, UCT Analogue Electronics and Devices, Wits Electronics I/II, Imperial Analysis and Design of Circuits).
- Digital logic/design with a lab (UIUC ECE 120/385, NUS EE2026/CG2027, Imperial Digital and Computer Architecture, MIT 6.1910).
- Signals and systems (all six).
- Computer organisation and architecture, microprocessors/assembly (UIUC ECE 220, NUS CG2028/CG3207, Imperial Instruction Architectures and Compilers, Wits Microprocessors).
- Embedded systems and real-time (UCT Embedded Systems I/II, NUS CG2271 RTOS, Imperial Embedded Systems elective).
- Operating systems or systems programming (UIUC ECE 391, MIT 6.1810 optional, NUS CG2271, Imperial Software Systems).
- Networks/communications (NUS EE4204, Imperial Communications, Wits Communication Fundamentals, UCT Communication & Network Engineering elective).
- Control (Imperial, UCT, Wits, MIT elective).
- Software engineering (NUS CS2113, Imperial Software Systems, Wits Software Development I to III).
- Machine learning (NUS CG3201 and EE2211 required; Imperial electives; UIUC advanced computing electives).
- Information security (NUS CS2107 required; Imperial/Wits electives).
- Capstone design (all six) and professional practice/ethics (NUS, UCT, Imperial).

## 6. Prerequisite chains

Verified in this session:
- MIT 6.004/6.1910 Computation Structures needs only physics 8.02 and basic programming (OCW syllabus), with 6.1900 (low-level C and assembly) as corequisite since 2023.
- UCT: EEE3099S (Design: Mechatronics) needs EEE3088F; EEE4120F High Performance Digital Embedded Systems needs EEE3096S (Embedded Systems II); EEE4119F Mechatronics needs MEC2047F, MEC2045S, EEE3094S. Embedded Systems I (year 2) leads to Embedded Systems II (year 3) to HPDES (year 4).
- IIT Delhi Advanced Computer Architecture needs second-year Computer Architecture; Cambridge R265 needs an undergraduate architecture course.
- UCT CS 3rd-year electives need CSC2004Z Programming Assessment.

Typical chains [general]:
- Programming (C) to Data Structures and Algorithms to Operating Systems to Distributed/Advanced OS.
- Basic Electronics to Circuit Analysis to Analogue Electronics and Devices to VLSI.
- Digital Logic to Computer Organisation to Assembly/Microprocessors to Computer Architecture to Advanced Computer Architecture to Hardware Accelerators.
- Calculus plus Linear Algebra to Signals and Systems to DSP and Control.
- Embedded Systems to Real-Time Systems to Cyber-Physical Systems.
- Networking I to Networking II or Data Communications to SDN/Wireless.

## 7. Representative weekly schedules

### 7.1 Bachelor core: MIT 6.004 Computation Structures (OCW, Spring 2017) [page]
Format: two 1-hour lectures and two 1-hour recitations per week. Assessment: 4 quizzes (120 pts), 7 labs (75 pts, labs 1, 3, 6, 7 mandatory), design project (20 pts), no final. The page gives lecture order, not dated weeks, so lectures are grouped here into suggested weeks.

| Week | Topics |
|---|---|
| 1 | Basics of information; the digital abstraction |
| 2 | CMOS; combinational logic |
| 3 | Sequential logic; finite state machines |
| 4 | Performance measures; design tradeoffs |
| 5 | Designing an instruction set; assembly language and models of computation |
| 6 | Compilers; procedures and stacks |
| 7 | Building a simple RISC processor (the Beta) |
| 8 | Caches and the memory hierarchy |
| 9 | Pipelining the processor |
| 10 | Virtual memory; virtualising the processor |
| 11 | Devices and interrupts |
| 12 | Concurrency and synchronisation |
| 13 | System-level communication; parallel processing; design project due |

### 7.3 Doctoral/graduate seminar: Cambridge R265 Advanced Topics in Computer Architecture [page]
Eight-week reading seminar, 16 contact hours, class limit 15. Each two-hour session: three student presentations of 15 minutes plus 5 minutes of questions, about 30 minutes of discussion, then a 20-minute lecture introducing next week's topic. Students not presenting write a 1,500-word essay comparing two of the three papers. Seven assessed exercises, lowest dropped. The page lists seven topics without dating them:

| Session | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1 | Trends in computer architecture |
| 2 | State-of-the-art microprocessor design |
| 3 | Memory system design |
| 4 | Hardware reliability |
| 5 | Specification, verification and test |
| 6 | Hardware security |
| 7 | Hardware accelerators and accelerators for machine learning |

A comparable doctoral-preparation format (two papers per session, 15 minute talk plus 20 minute discussion, written review before each session, research time with instructor feedback) was described for a US "Topics in Computer Architecture" course [snippet].

## 9. Proposed structure

### Bachelor (4 years, 28 core plus 6 of 11 electives; pool 39)
Core (suggested study order): calculus, linear-algebra-for-data-science, discrete-mathematics, probability-and-statistics, principles-of-programming, programming-with-c, basic-electronics, circuit-analysis, *Analogue Electronics and Devices* (new), digital-logic-design, data-structures-and-algorithms, signals-and-systems, *Electromagnetics for Engineers* (new), *Hardware Description Languages and FPGA Design* (new), computer-organization-and-architecture, assembly-language-and-microprocessors, embedded-systems, operating-systems, data-communications, computer-networking-i, control-systems, real-time-systems, digital-signal-processing, software-engineering, information-security, vlsi-design, internet-of-things, *Computer Engineering Capstone Project* (new).

Electives (6 required): computer-networking-ii, programming-with-cpp, mathematics-for-computer-science, database-systems-and-design, artificial-intelligence, machine-learning, sensors-and-actuators, numerical-methods-and-computation, wireless-communication, computer-graphics, compiler-construction.

### Master's (1 to 2 years, 5 core plus 4 of 10 electives; pool 15)
Core: Graduate Research Methods (new), Advanced Computer Architecture (new), Advanced Operating Systems (new), Hardware Accelerators and Custom Computing (new), Cyber-Physical Systems (new).
Electives (4 required): distributed-systems, big-data-and-parallel-computing, deep-learning, computer-vision, cryptography-and-network-security, software-defined-networking, malware-analysis-and-reverse-engineering, cloud-computing, mobile-robotics-and-navigation, theory-of-computation.
Milestones: coursework with one depth area; semester design project; optional industry internship or research placement (ETH-style); master's thesis or capstone with oral defence.

### PhD (4 to 5 years, 4 core plus 3 of 5 electives; pool 9)
Core: Doctoral Research Seminar in Computer Engineering (new), Advanced Topics in Computer Architecture (new), Graduate Research Methods, Advanced Computer Architecture (waived for students who completed it).
Electives (3 required): Advanced Operating Systems, Hardware Accelerators and Custom Computing, Cyber-Physical Systems, distributed-systems, deep-learning.
Milestones: qualifying examination (by end of semester 3 or 4), research committee formed, dissertation proposal defence at least one year before the final defence, teaching or tutoring experience, annual progress report, pre-defence committee meeting, dissertation and public defence.

New courses: 4 bachelor, 5 master's (Graduate Research Methods shared with other programmes), 2 PhD. Details are in `proposals/computer-engineering.json` in the session notes folder.
